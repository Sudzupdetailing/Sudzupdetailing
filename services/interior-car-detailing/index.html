What is actually included

The Sudz Quick Clean covers a complete interior vacuum, vinyl, rubber and plastic treatment across the hard surfaces, spot stain removal, door jambs cleaned, and windows cleaned inside and out.

That list is short because we would rather do five things properly than list fifteen and rush them. The order matters as much as the contents. We work top down and dry to wet, because cleaning a dashboard after you have shampooed the carpet just puts dust back onto a damp surface.

The vacuum is not the quick part

Most of the time on an interior detail goes into the vacuum, and most of that goes into places a normal vacuum never reaches.

Seats get moved fully forward and fully back so we can get at the rails and the carpet underneath, which is where the majority of debris in any vehicle collects. Floor mats come out and get done separately, and the carpet under them gets cleaned — that surface is frequently dirtier than the mat that was covering it. We use compressed air to blow debris out of seat seams, seat belt buckle recesses, centre console gaps and air vents before vacuuming, because a vacuum nozzle cannot generate airflow inside a 3mm gap.

Cargo areas get the load floor lifted and the spare wheel well cleared. On SUVs and estates, that space collects an extraordinary amount of grit and it is almost never touched.

Vinyl, rubber and plastic treatment

VRP treatment is the step people misunderstand most. It is not a shine product. Its job is to clean the hard surfaces properly and then put a protective layer back onto plastics that lose plasticiser to UV over time.

Untreated dashboards in Wisconsin have a hard life. Sun through the windscreen for years dries the surface out, and once it goes chalky and starts to crack, no product reverses it. Regular treatment slows that considerably.

We apply it to a matte finish rather than a wet gloss. A glossy dash reflects into the windscreen and is genuinely distracting when the sun is low, which in a Wisconsin winter afternoon is most of the drive home.

Spot stain removal, and what that means honestly

Spot stain removal in the Quick Clean means targeted treatment of individual marks — a coffee ring, a scuff on a door card, a patch on a seat base. It is not a full shampoo and extraction of every surface.

Whether a stain lifts depends on what it is and how long it has been there. Water-based spills caught within days usually come out completely. Coffee and soda respond well. Grease and oil need a solvent approach and often come out fully. Ink, dye transfer from clothing, and heavy chlorophyll staining from grass are the hardest, and sometimes the honest answer is a substantial improvement rather than complete removal.

If a vehicle has widespread staining rather than a few spots, we will tell you before we start that it needs more than the Quick Clean covers, and quote it properly.

Door jambs and glass — the tells

Door jambs are the single clearest indicator of whether a detail was done properly. They are what you look at every time you open the door, and they are what a car wash never touches. Cleaning them takes minutes and it changes the whole impression of the vehicle.

Interior glass is the other tell. Windscreens develop a film from plastic off-gassing combined with road dust, and it is the reason oncoming headlights scatter and dazzle at night. We do interior glass in two stages, because a single pass with a household spray usually smears the film into something worse than it started.
