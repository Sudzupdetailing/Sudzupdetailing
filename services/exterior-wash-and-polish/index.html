Why a drive-through wash is not the same thing

An automatic wash puts the same spinning material across every vehicle that came before yours, carrying whatever grit came off them. That is the mechanism behind the fine circular swirl marks you see on a dark car in direct sunlight.

A proper hand wash controls that. Dirt gets rinsed off before anything touches the paint, wash media is kept clean between panels, and the dirtiest areas — lower panels, behind the wheels — are done last so their grit does not get carried up onto cleaner paint.

This matters more on dark colours and on older single-stage paint, where swirls show immediately. It matters less on light silver, where they are nearly invisible. We are not going to pretend it is life-changing on a white work truck, but on a black car it is the difference between clean and properly clean.

Wisconsin-specific: brine, not just salt

Wisconsin road treatment includes liquid brine applied ahead of storms. It is worth understanding because it changes when you should wash.

Brine goes down on dry roads before the weather arrives. It dries into a white film that is easy to miss on a light-coloured vehicle. Because it is hygroscopic, it pulls moisture from the air and stays chemically active long after the road is dry — which means a car parked in a heated garage after a brine run is actively corroding while it sits.

The practical consequence is that winter washing is not cosmetic maintenance, it is corrosion control. The areas that matter most are the ones nobody looks at: inside the wheel arches, along the rocker panels, in the door jambs and around the tailgate seams. Our exterior work covers jambs and arches specifically because that is where brine sits.

What polish does and does not do

Polish in our VIP service is a finishing step. It cleans up light surface contamination and leaves a uniform, protected finish with real gloss.

It is important to be clear about the ceiling here. This is not multi-stage machine paint correction, and it will not remove deep scratches, sanding marks or failed clear coat. If you can catch a fingernail in a scratch, polish will not remove it. What it does do is dramatically improve a finish that has gone dull from accumulated road film, light oxidation and organic deposit — and that describes most vehicles over three years old in this climate.

The improvement is often most dramatic on vehicles that live outside under trees. Years of pollen, sap film and dust build a layer that owners mistake for the paint having faded. Frequently it has not.

Wheels and tires

Wheels take more chemical and thermal abuse than any other part of a vehicle. Brake dust is hot metallic particulate that physically embeds into wheel finish, and if it is left across a whole winter it starts to pit the coating.

We clean wheel faces, barrels where accessible, and the tires themselves before dressing them. Tire dressing goes on properly rather than being sprayed on and left to sling black streaks down your paint on the first drive — which is what happens with the aerosol approach, and it is why we apply and wipe rather than apply and walk away.
