Recreation country is hard on interiors

Kewaskum sits at the north end of the county with the Kettle Moraine State Forest, the Eisenbahn State Trail, Sunburst ski hill, Hon-E-Kor golf course and the lakes at Mauthe and Long all within easy reach. Camping, hiking and the Ice Age Trail are minutes away.

Every one of those activities puts something specific in a vehicle. Trail mud and boot grit in the footwells. Bike wheels and chain grease against the cargo carpet. Wet dogs, which is a category of its own. Golf shoes with soil packed into the spikes. Ski and board gear dripping meltwater through the whole cabin.

The hardest of these is dog. Dog hair works into fabric weave in a way that vacuuming alone does not resolve, and it needs mechanical agitation — rubber tooling and brushes — to bring it up before it can be extracted. The dander and the wet-dog smell are separate problems again, and both live in the seat foam rather than in the air.

Lake sand and the drying question

Long Lake and Mauthe Lake generate the same sand problem as Pike Lake does closer to us, and Kewaskum vehicles usually have more of it because the trips are longer and the gear is heavier.

The rule with sand is to get it out before it dries and grinds. Sand is abrasive. Every time you sit on a seat with dry sand in the weave, you are effectively sanding your own upholstery. Over a few summers this genuinely wears fabric thin on the seat base, and that is not something a detail can reverse.

The other Kewaskum reality is that gear gets stored in vehicles between outings. A wet towel or damp life jacket left in a closed cargo area for a week in July creates mildew in the carpet. If that has happened, tell us. Mildew needs a different treatment than ordinary soil and it is worth doing correctly the first time.

Worth planning around the drive

Kewaskum is about thirty minutes from our shop, the longest regular run we make. We are happy to do it, but we would suggest making it count with the full interior and exterior service rather than a quick refresh.

The natural rhythm for Kewaskum vehicles is twice a year: once in late spring after the salt season, and once in early autumn after the summer lake and trail season. That timing catches both of the seasonal problems at the point where they have finished rather than mid-cycle.
