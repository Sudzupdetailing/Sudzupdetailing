Auto Detailing for the Town of Erin, Wisconsin

Erin is rural Washington County at its most scenic, and it is home to Erin Hills, the public course that hosted the 2017 U.S. Open. Golf traffic gives its vehicles a specific signature.

Golf is surprisingly hard on a car

Erin Hills brought national attention to this corner of the county when it hosted the 2017 U.S. Open, and the course draws steady traffic through the season. Golf produces a very consistent set of vehicle problems and almost nobody anticipates them.

Shoes are the main one. Soft spikes pack soil and grass into the sole, and that goes straight into the driver's footwell and the cargo area. Grass staining on carpet is genuinely difficult because chlorophyll is a dye rather than a soil, and it binds to fibre. We can usually lift most of it, but a heavy fresh grass stain on light carpet may not come out completely, and we would rather say that upfront.

Bags in the cargo area are the second. A wet bag after a rain-shortened round sits in a closed boot and creates exactly the damp environment that produces a musty smell within days. Trolleys and pull carts scuff the cargo trim and load lip. And the cargo carpet takes the brunt of everything, which is why we treat it as a primary surface rather than an afterthought.

Country roads and the Kettle Moraine

Away from the course, Erin is winding rural road through Kettle Moraine terrain. That means more time on surfaces with loose material at the edges, and more overhanging tree cover than most of the county.

Tree cover is a mixed blessing. It keeps paint out of direct sun, which is good, but it means sap, bird residue and leaf litter, which is not. Vehicles parked routinely under trees in Erin come to us with a dulled finish caused by a long accumulation of organic deposit rather than by any single event. Those respond very well to a proper wash and polish — the improvement is often more dramatic than the owner expects.

Winter on Erin's back roads is a genuine consideration too. Township roads see less treatment than the state highways, which means more packed snow and less brine, but also more sand. Sand in the footwells through February is standard here.

What we suggest for Erin vehicles

If you golf regularly, an interior service at the end of the season is the one that matters. A season of soil, grass and damp bags in the cargo area is a real accumulation and it is far easier to reset once than to chase all summer.

For vehicles that live under trees, the full interior and exterior service once or twice a year does considerably more for the paint than people expect. The change after removing years of organic film is usually the thing customers comment on.
