Lake Michigan air

Lake Michigan air is harder on paint than people realise

Mequon sits a few miles from the shoreline, and proximity to a large body of water changes the exterior environment in ways drivers rarely connect to their vehicle.

Lake air carries more moisture, and the practical consequence is dwell time. Dew forms earlier and lingers longer on paint, and a surface that stays damp overnight gives whatever landed on it — pollen, fallout, bird droppings, tree debris — far more opportunity to bond or etch than the same contaminant would get on a dry vehicle inland.

Fog and lake-effect conditions do the same thing on a larger scale. This is why Mequon paint often carries more bonded contamination than a comparable car from further west, even when it is washed just as often.

Long private drives, and what comes off them

Large-lot zoning is the defining feature of the city, and it means a lot of Mequon vehicles start and end every trip on a long private driveway rather than pulling straight onto pavement.

Gravel and crushed-stone drives throw fine dust up into wheel arches and the lower panels, and it gets tracked into the cabin on shoes at both ends of every journey. Asphalt drives seal and re-seal over the years, and fresh sealer picks up on tyres and transfers onto rocker panels and mud flaps.

Mature tree cover across much of the city adds the rest: sap in summer, which is acidic enough to mark clear coat if it bakes, and leaf tannin in autumn, which stains light paint and blocks cowl drains.

Winter miles on I-43

Most Mequon commuting runs I-43 south toward Milwaukee, and interstate winter miles are heavier exposure than village driving.

Interstate lanes are treated first and most often, and at highway speed a vehicle travels through a continuous mist of brine thrown up by surrounding traffic rather than simply over it. That reaches the whole car, not just the lower panels.

Combined with lake humidity keeping surfaces damp longer, that is a genuinely corrosive combination, and it is why we push exterior protection on Ozaukee County vehicles ahead of winter rather than after it.
