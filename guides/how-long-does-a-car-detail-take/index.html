How Long Does a Car Detail Take?

The honest answer to this question is longer than most people want to hear, and a shop that gives you a shorter one is telling you what it is going to skip.

Interior detail: hours, and usually same-day

A proper interior on a typical car is one to two hours of work and it is usually a same-day collection. What sets the time is not the size of the car so much as what is in it: a commuter with a clean cabin is at the short end; a family vehicle with car seats, or a work truck with a year of a working life in it, is at the long end and can run longer.

The time goes into things that do not look like time. Compressed air through every seam before the vacuum. Seats moved fully forward and back. Mats out and done separately. Extraction on carpet that needs it, with dwell time between passes. Glass done twice. A shop quoting forty-five minutes for an interior is quoting a vacuum and a wipe.

Full detail: most of a working day

Interior plus exterior, done in the right order, is most of a day for one vehicle. The exterior half alone — wheels and arches, a two-bucket wash, chemical iron removal, a clay stage panel by panel, then protection — is several hours before the interior starts.

It is a drop-off, not a wait. We book one full detail a day rather than rotating between several, because dwell time and the second pass are where the result comes from and they are the first things cut when another car is waiting. Our full detail page covers why the order matters.

Odor removal: assessed, and it depends entirely

Odor work is the widest range there is. A cabin filter and something under a seat is an hour. Fish in a spare wheel well, a decade of smoke in a headliner, or a padding full of water is a day or more and may take a second visit for a second extraction pass.

We do not quote odor work over the phone because the time cannot be known until the source is found, and finding the source is often most of the job. What we will tell you before starting is which case you have and whether the honest outcome is elimination or improvement.

Paint correction and coating: days

Correction is not a one-day job on any car worth correcting. Decontamination, assessment under inspection light, a test spot, correction panel by panel, refinement, inspection, and then protection with a cure time the product actually needs. A single-stage correction and coating on a car in reasonable condition is two to three days in the shop. Multi-stage correction on a neglected or high-value finish is longer.

A shop quoting a full correction and coating in a day is either cutting stages or working fast enough to burn a panel. Our high-value car guide covers what to ask. The right answer to how long is always longer than you hoped, and that is the answer you want.
