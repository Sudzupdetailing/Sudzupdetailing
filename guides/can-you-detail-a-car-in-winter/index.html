Can You Detail a Car in Winter?

Yes, and for half the services it is the best time of year. For the other half it is the worst, and knowing which is which saves you a wasted visit.

What a heated bay changes

The reason mobile detailing is seasonal in Wisconsin is that water freezes and products do not work below a certain temperature. A shop with a heated bay does not have that problem: the car comes in, warms up, and every process that works in July works in January.

What the bay does not change is what the car goes back out into. A car that leaves a heated bay with freshly decontaminated, unprotected paint is back on salted roads within the hour, and that is the fact that decides which winter services are worth doing.

Interior work: best in winter

January is the strongest month of the year for interior detailing, and it is our quietest. The salt is actively sitting in the carpet backing; the moisture that came in on boots since December is at its worst; the musty smell that will be obvious in March is establishing now. Extraction in January removes all of that while it is happening rather than after it has done its damage.

Odor work, interior protection on a freshly cleaned cabin, leather conditioning, glass — all of it works as well or better in winter, and the cabin goes back out into the cold dry. The interior page and the month-by-month guide both push January for this reason.

Exterior decontamination: possible, mostly pointless

We can decontaminate paint in January. The iron remover works, the clay works, the car comes out clean. Then it drives home on brine and by the next morning the film is back. The work was done properly and it lasted a day.

The exception is a vehicle that is coated or about to be, or one going into storage. Otherwise, exterior decontamination waits for April, when the salt stops and the work holds. The same goes for correction: polish a car in January and it goes back out into the season most likely to re-mar it.

Coating: the one exterior job that argues for winter

A ceramic coating cures in a heated bay regardless of the month, and a car that leaves coated in January spends the rest of the winter with brine sitting on the coating rather than the clear coat. That is a real argument for doing it mid-winter rather than waiting, particularly on a new car or a car that has just been corrected and is otherwise going out bare.

The catch is the same as always: a coated car still needs rinsing through winter. Our winter washing guide covers when.
