Tesla Detailing

Tesla paint is the softest and thinnest we regularly work on, and that single fact changes the advice for everything else about the car.

The paint

Tesla clear coat is widely understood in the detailing trade to be softer and thinner than most European and many domestic finishes. Soft means it marks easily — a brush wash or a careless drying towel leaves visible marring on a Tesla that would not show on a Porsche. Thin means there is less clear coat to remove when correcting that marring, so correction has to be conservative and the number of corrections over the car's life is lower.

Practically: a Tesla that has been through automatic washes for two years usually has more visible swirling than a comparable-age car of most other makes, and correcting it fully may not be safe. We measure paint depth across every panel before touching one, and we will tell you where the paint says to stop rather than where the defect does.

Why coating early matters more here

On most cars a coating is a good idea. On a Tesla it is close to essential, and the reason is the paint above. A coated surface takes the contact that would otherwise mark the clear coat, and it releases dirt with far less scrubbing, which is what a soft finish needs most.

The timing argument is also stronger. A new Tesla needs decontamination and usually light correction for delivery marring, then a coating, and the job is close to the starting price. A two-year-old uncoated Tesla needs correction that has to be conservative because of the thickness, and the result is limited by how much can safely come off. Coat it in the first month if at all possible.

The interior

Tesla interiors use a synthetic leather rather than hide, and it behaves differently. It does not need conditioning — conditioners intended for real leather sit on it and attract dirt. It is more resistant to staining than hide but it marks from dye transfer, particularly from dark denim onto light seats, and that transfer sets if it is left.

The minimalist cabin means one large screen and a lot of piano-black and matte surfaces, all of which show fingerprints and scratch under anything abrasive. They get dedicated products and their own cloths.

The glass roof

A large glass roof is not paint and does not get treated as paint — no clay, no polish. What it does do is collect the same fallout and mineral spotting a roof panel would, and hard-water rings on tinted glass are obvious from inside. It gets a glass-specific decontamination and a glass sealant, which keeps water sheeting off it.

Our coating page covers the process and the correction page covers why clear coat is finite. On a Tesla both apply with more force than usual.
